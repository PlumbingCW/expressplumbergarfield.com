The area.

Where Plauderville sits in Garfield.

The City of Garfield's history page says Plauderville Avenue was named after Plauder Ecke, which means Gossip Corner, a German language newspaper from Brooklyn. The same page says the area was also known as Bear's Nest. Both names show that Plauderville has been in use as a local name for a long time.

Today the name is easiest to find at the Plauderville station on NJ Transit's Bergen County Line, which sits on the Saddle Brook border, and on Plauderville Avenue, which appears on the city's street index. We found no published boundary for Plauderville, so we describe it by those anchors and not by exact blocks.

Shared buildings.

Two-family homes and shared plumbing.

A local real estate guide describes two-family homes near the Plauderville station. If you own or rent in a building with more than one unit, a few habits make any plumbing call faster. This is general plumbing information and not a claim about any one house.

Learn where each unit's shutoff valve is and whether the building also has a main shutoff in the cellar. When a pipe fails upstairs, the household below is often the first to see water, so keep the owner's number and your neighbors' numbers handy.

Drains can be shared as well. If a kitchen sink in one unit backs up, the stoppage may sit in a branch line that serves other units. Tell us which fixtures in which units are slow, because that points to where the blockage is.

Renters should ask the owner or manager before booking a repair, since many leases require approval. Have that person's name ready when you call.

The water supply and what to ask the city.

According to Garfield's 2018 water quality report, the city's wells and water bought from the Passaic Valley Water Commission blend at the Belmont Hill Tank. It describes the north end of the city as receiving mostly well water, the south end mostly Passaic Valley water, and the area between as mostly blended water. It lists no streets, so it cannot tell you which supply reaches a given house.

Ask the Garfield water department which supply serves your address. The answer matters for scale on fixtures and heaters, and the report states that hardness causes scaling of pipe. Our Belmont page lists the hardness figures in that report.

More recently, the city says it finished PFAS treatment on all 16 of its wells and returned to compliance with the PFOA and PFOS limits in July 2024. That is a citywide water quality notice. It says nothing about the pipes inside your home.

Three checks you can do today.

Find your main water shutoff and confirm that it turns. Read the age label on your water heater. Look under sinks for damp cabinet floors. These are general checks that suit any home and are not a claim about any one street.
