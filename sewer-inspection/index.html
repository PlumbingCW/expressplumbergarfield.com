Sewer Camera Inspection in Garfield, NJ.

A sewer camera inspection shows the inside of your drain line on video, so you can see cracks, roots, offsets and bellies before they turn into a backup. It is a planned service, and it is often the best money spent before a purchase or a repair.

If sewage is already coming up through a drain, start with our emergency sewer pages instead, and we will add the camera once the line is open.

What a sewer camera inspection shows.

A small waterproof camera on a flexible rod goes in through a cleanout and runs toward the street. It records the pipe material, joints, cracks, roots, scale, low spots that hold water and any section that has shifted. A locator can mark the depth and position on the surface.

You get the footage and a plain language summary. No one has to guess whether the pipe is the problem, and you do not pay to dig up a yard that is fine. According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs, you can look up a plumber's license online before you hire, and we suggest you do that for whoever you let dig into a sewer line afterward.

When to look.

Good reasons to schedule an inspection.

Buying a house.

A seller's disclosure rarely covers what is under the lawn. A camera shows the lateral's condition before closing.

Repeat clogs.

If cables keep clearing the same drain, the camera shows why.

Before finishing a basement.

A hidden leak or broken pipe under a new floor is costly to reach later.

After a flood.

Saturated ground can shift or crack old pipe.

Slow drains throughout the house.

A belly or buildup in the main may be the cause.

Wet spots in the yard.

Surface water or lush grass along the pipe route can mean a leak.

How an inspection runs.

1.

Access.

We open a cleanout, or pull a toilet or reach a vent if there is none.

2.

Run the camera.

We push the camera through the pipe while recording and noting distance.

3.

Locate.

If a defect appears, we mark its position and depth at the surface.

4.

Review.

You see the footage, hear what we found and get options in plain terms.

Why the camera matters in an older city.

Garfield was built out long ago, and much of its housing stock predates modern pipe. A house on Midland Avenue or in Plauderville may have a clay or cast iron lateral from the street. Those pipes can be sound after eighty years or failing after thirty, depending on soil and use.

The only way to tell is to look. The cost of an inspection is small next to a surprise repair, and the footage becomes a record you can hand to a future buyer or an insurer.

What we will not do with the footage.

We do not use a camera to sell a repair you do not need. If the pipe is fine, we say so and you keep the recording. If it is not, you see exactly where and why, and you can take the footage to another contractor for a second price if you want.
