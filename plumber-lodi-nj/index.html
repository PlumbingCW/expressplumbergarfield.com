Before We Arrive.

Find the main shutoff.

It is usually in the cellar near the meter. Turn it clockwise to stop water to the house.

Clear a path.

Move boxes and furniture away from the cellar stairs and the area around the leak or heater.

Stay clear of electricity.

Do not stand in water near the panel, outlets or appliances. Shut off power only if the panel is dry.

Photograph the damage.

Take photos before moving anything. Your insurer will ask for them.

Flood-prone streets and a rising Saddle River.

Parts of Lodi flood in heavy rain, and the Saddle River runs through the area. The National Weather Service records the river at Lodi cresting at 13.94 feet in September 1999 and 13.5 feet in August 2011, with a flood stage of 6 feet. Basements in low spots took on water in both events.

If your cellar floods, the pump and the discharge route matter. A battery backup keeps a pump going when the power fails. See basement waterproofing for the longer term plan.

What Lodi homeowners can do now.

Check your water heater's age plate and your shutoff valves while the weather is calm. A valve that will not turn today will not turn in an emergency, and an old tank leaks without warning. If either is a concern, call and we will talk it through.

Older pipes, newer problems.

Lodi has more than 10,000 homes, and they were built across many decades. A house with original galvanized supply pipe behaves very differently from one with copper or PEX. If your hot water pressure has faded while cold stays strong, the hot line is often the first to narrow with scale.

That pattern is a reason to book a look before a leak starts. Our technicians can trace which lines are narrowing and tell you whether a repair or a repipe is the sensible next step. You decide with a written price in hand.
