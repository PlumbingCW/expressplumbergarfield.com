Emergency plumber.

What an emergency plumber in Garfield actually does.

An emergency plumber stops damage first and repairs second. That means closing a main valve, clearing a main line, isolating a burst pipe or shutting down a failing water heater so water stops moving. Once the house is safe, we talk about the repair and the price.

Our 24/7 line is staffed around the clock. When you call, say where the problem is, how fast water is moving and whether anyone is hurt or smells gas. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility first.

Know the signs.

What counts as a plumbing emergency.

  • A burst or leaking pipe that does not stop when you close the fixture valve.
  • Sewage or dirty water backing up through drains or toilets. See sewer line cleaning.
  • A failed water heater that is leaking or gives no hot water in cold weather. See water heater replacement.
  • A basement taking on water during a storm. See sump pump repair.
  • No heat from a boiler in freezing weather. See boiler repair.

Plumbing across Belmont, Bogart Heights and Plauderville.

Garfield is a city of 2.19 square miles in Bergen County, with named neighborhoods such as Belmont, Bogart Heights and Plauderville. Housing runs from single family homes to two and three family buildings, and many are old. Older houses mean older drains, older supply pipe and cellars that need attention.

Streets near the Passaic River and the Saddle River, such as River Drive and the blocks near Dundee Dam, are the ones where wet cellars are most likely. If your basement ever gets damp in heavy rain, plan a sump pump check before the next storm.

Garfield water and why scale matters.

The City of Garfield's 2018 water quality report describes a supply that blends the city's own wells with treated water from the Passaic Valley Water Commission, mixed at the Belmont Hill Tank. It reports hardness above the recommended range and says that hardness scales pipes. For a homeowner, that means scale in water heaters, aerators and fixtures.

A yearly flush of a tank heater and a look at your shutoff valves is cheap insurance. If your heater is more than ten years old and starts to rumble or leak, plan for a replacement before it fails at night.

Roads, rails and getting to you fast.

Garfield's main roads, Midland Avenue, Passaic Street, River Drive and County Route 507, tie neighborhoods together, and U.S. Route 46 passes through. Garfield Station and Plauderville Station serve NJ Transit's Bergen County Line and Main Line.

Our base at 174 Passaic Street sits on the main commercial road, which keeps most Garfield addresses within a short drive. Dispatch times depend on traffic and where the nearest technician is, so we give you an update when we send someone.

Licensed work and what that means.

New Jersey law limits plumbing work to a licensed master plumber or contractors working under one, according to the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. You can check any plumber's license through the Division of Consumer Affairs.

Permits for larger jobs go through the City of Garfield building department. We tell you when a permit applies before the work starts.
