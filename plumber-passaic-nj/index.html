Emergency plumbing in a dense city.

Passaic has about 70,500 residents on 3.2 square miles, which is a lot of people in a small area. Multi family buildings dominate, from older walk ups to towers, and the Passaic River forms its eastern border, across from Garfield, Wallington and Rutherford.

In a building with several units, one leak or backup can affect everyone. We ask who is affected, where the water is coming from and who controls the shutoff, so we can stop the problem quickly without turning off more than needed. Water, lead and the Passaic Valley Water Commission.

The Passaic Valley Water Commission is owned by the cities of Paterson, Clifton and Passaic, and it serves the area. PVWC reported completing a lead service line replacement program, with nearly 5,000 lines removed across its service area. If your building is older, ask the landlord or PVWC about the status of your line.

Lead can also come from household plumbing inside the building. A tenant or owner who is concerned can run the cold tap for a minute after the water has sat, and ask about testing. Call us if you want to replace old fixtures or valves.

For landlords and managers.

Know where each shutoff is, and keep a list posted in the basement. In a multi unit building, the minute saved by knowing the right valve is often the difference between one wet ceiling and four.

Making a shutoff map for your building.

A one page map showing the main water shutoff, the water heater, the boiler and each unit's shutoff takes an hour to make. Post it in the cellar and share a copy with tenants. When a pipe fails, anyone on the block can then close the right valve.

Add the phone number for the emergency line and the name of the contact who has the keys. Our dispatcher can use that information to get a technician inside quickly.
