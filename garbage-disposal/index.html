Garbage disposal trouble, in the order it happens.

A disposal fails in a few predictable ways. It jams, and the motor hums. It leaks from the sink flange, the body or the drain connection. The motor dies. Or the sink backs up because the disposal outlet or the line past it is clogged.

Each has a different fix, and some are quick. A jam often clears with a hex key turned at the bottom of the unit. A leak from the body, by contrast, almost always means the disposal needs replacing. According to the City of Garfield's 2018 water quality report, hardness is above the recommended range, so a disposal drain that has scaled up clogs sooner than a clean one.

Urgent signs.

When a disposal problem is an emergency.

  • Water is leaking from the disposal body into the cabinet.
  • The sink is full and not draining and the disposal will not run.
  • A burning smell or smoke comes from the unit. Switch it off at the breaker and call.
  • The motor hums, and the lights flicker or the breaker trips.
  • It is the only kitchen sink and you cannot cook or clean.

What a disposal should and should not handle.

Disposals are made for soft food scraps and small amounts of waste. They are not made for grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, fibrous vegetables like celery, potato peels in bulk, bones or pasta that swells. Those cause jams and clogs, and grease coats the pipes downstream.

In older Garfield kitchens, the drain from the sink is often small and may have scale inside from years of hard water, as the city's 2018 water quality report suggests with its above range hardness. A disposal puts more waste into a pipe that may already be narrow, so a repeat clog is a sign to call a drain cleaner too. See drain cleaning.

Choosing a replacement disposal.

Size the motor to the household. A half horsepower unit suits a single person or a small family. Three quarter or one horsepower units handle more volume and are quieter. Continuous feed models are the most common. Batch feed models run only with the stopper in place, which some families prefer for safety.

Check the mounting. Most new disposals fit most sinks, but older sinks and stainless units can need an adapter. If a dishwasher drains through the disposal, a new knockout plug must be removed. We handle that.
