Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Garfield, NJ.

Basics.

What the service line is and where it fails.

The service line runs from the water main under the street to a shutoff and meter inside your cellar. Depending on the age of the house, it can be lead, galvanized steel, copper or plastic. Joints at the main and at the foundation wall fail more often than the straight pipe between them.

A failing line shows up as low pressure, discolored water after repairs, a wet spot on the lawn or a quiet hiss near the meter. Because the pipe is buried, small leaks can run a long time unseen. According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs, you can look up a plumber's license online before you hire, and we suggest you do that for service line work.

Urgent signs.

When a service line leak is an emergency.

  • Water is breaking through the lawn, driveway or sidewalk.
  • Pressure has dropped sharply at every fixture, and no one in the neighborhood is working on the main.
  • The meter runs with every fixture closed.
  • The cellar wall where the pipe enters is wet or water is coming in around it.
  • The main valve will not close.

Lead and old pipe material.

Lead service lines are a concern wherever they exist. New Jersey requires water systems to inventory and replace lead service lines, and water suppliers publish information about their lines. Ask the City of Garfield or the Passaic Valley Water Commission whether your line is on the inventory, and check the pipe where it enters your cellar.

A scratch test can tell lead from copper or galvanized steel. Lead is soft and dull gray, and it shines silver when scratched. If you suspect lead, run the cold tap for a minute before drinking and talk to us about replacement.

Repair or replace the line.

We locate the problem first, so you know whether a short repair is enough.

Repair a section.

  • Fits a single break or bad joint.
  • Less digging and lower cost.
  • Leaves the rest of the old pipe in place.
  • Best when the pipe is otherwise sound.

Replace the whole line.

  • Fits pinholes in several spots or old material.
  • Removes lead and galvanized pipe.
  • Higher cost and more excavation.
  • Fewer repeat repairs.

Who is responsible for which section.

In most systems, the utility owns the main and the pipe up to a curb stop, and the homeowner owns the line from there to the house. The dividing point differs between systems, so ask your water supplier or the City of Garfield. Public Works can tell you where the shutoff is.

If the leak is on the utility's side, report it to them. If it is yours, we repair it. We will tell you what we find, and the local rules can be confirmed with the building department before we start.
