When to call an emergency plumber in Elmwood Park.

  • Floor drains bubbling or sewage in a tub or shower.
  • A pipe leak that does not stop when you close a fixture valve.
  • A water heater leaking or a rotten egg smell with no hot water.
  • Water through a basement wall or floor after rain.
  • Frozen pipes with no flow from a tap in a hard freeze.

Elmwood Park by the numbers, and what they mean for plumbing.

Elmwood Park is a borough of about 2.7 square miles with 21,422 residents at the 2020 census and nearly 8,000 homes. The Passaic River runs along its western edge, across from Paterson and Clifton, and Garfield lies to the south. Route 4, U.S. Route 46, Interstate 80 and the Garden State Parkway cross it.

A borough this size has housing from several eras, and that means a range of pipe ages. Ask what your own house has, because older drains and supply lines are the ones that fail first. A homeowner who knows what they have can plan a repair before a failure. According to federal drinking water records, Elmwood Park's supply is purchased surface water, so confirm the supplier for your own street with the borough.

Who supplies your water.

Federal records list Elmwood Park's supply as purchased surface water, which means the borough buys treated water from another system. The supplier can differ by street, so call the borough water department and ask who serves your address before assuming anything about hardness or service line material.

This matters when you shop for a water heater or a softener. A heater in soft water lasts longer than one in hard water, and we recommend a different maintenance plan for each. When you have the answer, tell the dispatcher and we will plan around it.

A tip for Elmwood Park basements.

If you have a floor drain, pour a gallon of water into it twice a year. A dry trap lets sewer gas into the house, and a drain that gurgles tells you the line needs attention. If it does gurgle, call us.
