Urgent first steps.

Sewer backup: what to do first.

A main line blockage is different from a sink clog. All the plumbing in the building drains into one pipe, so when it stops, the lowest fixtures show it first. In most Garfield houses that is a cellar floor drain, a basement shower or a laundry standpipe.

Stop the water, close the lids on floor drains, and open a window if there is an odor. Do not try to snake the main yourself. A cable machine that is not controlled can break an old pipe or hurt your hands. According to the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, sewer line work must be done by a licensed master plumber or someone working under one, so ask to see a license before you agree to a price.

The cleanout is the plumber's door.

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the sewer line. Most houses have one in the cellar near where the main leaves the foundation, and some have a second in the yard. If you can find it, we can usually clear the line without touching a fixture.

If your house has no cleanout, we install one. It costs less than repeated emergency calls, and it lets the next technician work faster. Tell us whether the main sits under a finished floor and we will plan the access with you.

Roots, grease and old pipe in Garfield.

Many Garfield lines were laid decades ago in clay, cast iron or pitch fiber. Roots find the joints of clay lines easily, and cast iron scales and narrows from the inside. Both leave a rough surface where paper and grease catch.

If you are on a corner lot near the Passaic River or along River Drive, ground movement and high water tables can shift old pipe. We note any offset on the camera so you know whether it is a maintenance item or a repair job.

Who owns the sewer line.

In most New Jersey towns the homeowner owns the lateral from the house to the connection at the street main, while the municipality owns the main itself. The exact point varies, so we advise you to ask Garfield public works or the building department where your property responsibility ends.

If the blockage is in the municipal main, we can often tell by whether the neighbors on your block have the same symptoms. In that case, you report it to the city instead of paying for repair on a pipe that is not yours.
