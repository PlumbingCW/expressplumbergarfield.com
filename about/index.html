About us.

What we do.

We handle residential and small commercial plumbing problems, from burst pipes and drain backups to water heater failures and sump pumps. We list the full set on our services page, and we say plainly when a job is outside what a call and a visit can solve.

Our base is 174 Passaic Street in Garfield, on one of the city's busiest streets. From there, we reach Lodi, Elmwood Park, Wallington, Saddle Brook and Passaic, all within a short drive in normal traffic.

How the emergency line works.

A person answers the phone. They ask where the problem is, what you can see and what you can safely shut off. Then they dispatch the nearest free technician and tell you what to expect.

We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic, weather and call volume change it. We do promise to tell you what is happening and to give you a price before work begins.

How we price.

Every job has different parts, access and labor. We quote after a technician has seen the problem, in plain terms. You approve the price before we start. If the job grows, we stop and talk to you before going on.

We do not quote a number from a photograph when gas, venting or sewer work is involved. A wrong number early is worse than an honest one after a look.

Licensing, permits and what to check.

In New Jersey, plumbing work is done by a licensed master plumber or by people who work under one, as the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers sets out. The Division of Consumer Affairs lets you look up any plumber.

For permits, the City of Garfield building department handles filings at 111 Outwater Lane. We tell you if your job needs one.

What we will not do.

We will not push a repair you do not need. If a camera shows a sound pipe, we tell you so. If an old heater can be repaired for less than a replacement, we say that too.

We will not tell you a price is lower than it is, hide fees until the end or promise results we cannot control. If a job is outside our scope, we tell you.

The Garfield we serve.

Garfield sits on the Passaic River in Bergen County, with named neighborhoods such as Belmont, Bogart Heights and Plauderville. The housing is mixed and often old, with many two and three family buildings. That shapes the calls we get: aging drains, cellar floods and water heaters in tight basements.

We write our service pages with that in mind, and we point to the sources we use, such as the city's own water quality report. If you want to know more about a specific job, see the pages for drain cleaning, water heater replacement or leak detection.

A note on being new to the neighborhood.

Customers sometimes ask how long a plumbing company has been around. We would rather show you than tell you. Read the pages, check the sources they cite, verify any license with the state and judge us on how the phone call goes.

That is the only fair test for a service you may need at two in the morning. If the call goes badly, hang up and call someone else. If it goes well, you will know within a minute.

Standards we hold ourselves to.

Answer the phone, tell the truth about what we see and put the price in front of you before work begins. Those three habits explain most of what people value in a local plumber, and they cost nothing to keep.

We also keep the website honest. Pages cite public sources, such as Garfield's own water quality report, and say "ask your utility" when a figure is not something we can verify. You will not find invented reviews, fake staff photos or made up statistics here.
