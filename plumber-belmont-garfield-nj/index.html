The area.

The Belmont name and where it sits.

According to the City of Garfield's history page, the name came from an octagon shaped house whose owner called it Belmont because it resembled a bell, and the name then spread to a section of the city. The same page says the Passaic and New York Railroad's station stood at the foot of Belmont Avenue.

Today Belmont is easiest to find by Belmont Avenue, Belmont Hill County Park and the Belmont Oval Little League field, all of which appear on the city's street and park maps. Bergen County describes the park as sitting on Belmont Hill, with a playground and a scenic overlook that looks toward Paterson. We found no published boundary for Belmont, so we describe it by those anchors.

Water heaters.

Water heater signs to act on.

  • Popping or rumbling from the tank, which is often sediment on the bottom. See water heater repair.
  • Rust colored hot water or a metallic taste only when the hot tap runs. A tank near the end of its life may need water heater replacement.
  • Water pooling at the base of the tank. Shut the supply valve and call.
  • Lukewarm water or a heater that runs out quickly. A failed element or a scaled burner can cause it.
  • A tankless unit with error codes or weak flow. See tankless water heater service.
Water quality.

Hard water and your water heater.

The city's 2018 report lists a recommended hardness range of 50 to 250 ppm. It gives results of 356 ppm and 436 ppm for the city's well sources and 92 to 160 ppm for water bought from the Passaic Valley Water Commission. The report says hardness causes scaling of pipe and is not a health concern.

It reports no hardness figure for Belmont itself. The report says wells and purchased water blend at the Belmont Hill Tank, so ask the Garfield water department which supply your street receives. If you see white crust on faucets or your heater rumbles, scale is a reasonable suspect, whatever your supply.

Scale insulates heating elements and burner surfaces, so a heater works harder and may wear sooner. A yearly drain and flush of a tank is cheap maintenance. A tankless unit needs descaling on its own schedule, so keep the maker's manual handy.

Slopes, cellars and sump pumps.

Belmont Hill is a hill, and the city's elevation range is wide. Homes lower on a slope can receive runoff from higher ground, and a cellar drain or sump discharge needs somewhere to go. These are general points. We found no source that describes flooding in Belmont, so check the FEMA flood map for your own address before you decide anything.

If you already have a pump, test it by pouring water into the pit and watching it start. A battery backup keeps it running through an outage. Ask the building department at 111 Outwater Lane where a sump discharge is allowed to go. See sump pump service for how a repair or replacement works.

Local note.

Flush the tap after repairs.

The city's report advises running a tap for 30 seconds to 2 minutes before drinking or cooking when water has sat in the pipes. The advice is aimed at lead exposure, and it is a good habit after any repair, because loosened debris and air clear out of the line.
