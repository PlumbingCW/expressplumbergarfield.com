Commercial plumbing in Garfield, built around your hours.

Business plumbing is different from home plumbing. Fixtures see far more use, grease and debris loads are heavier and a shutdown costs revenue. We schedule work around your open hours where we can, and we answer emergencies at any hour.

Passaic Street and Midland Avenue carry most of Garfield's storefronts, many in older buildings with shared drains and cellar mains. A backup in one unit can affect its neighbors, so we confirm the building layout before we shut anything off. According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs, you can look up a plumber's license online before you hire, and we suggest you do that for a commercial plumbing contractor.

Urgent signs.

When a business plumbing problem is an emergency.

  • Floor drains or grease lines are backing up in a kitchen or prep area.
  • A burst pipe or a failed water heater is flooding the space.
  • No hot water in a restaurant, salon or clinic where it is needed to operate.
  • Restrooms are out of service and customers cannot be served.
  • Sewage odor or overflow in a customer facing area.

Grease and maintenance.

Restaurants lose more hours to grease than to any other plumbing problem. A grease trap or interceptor needs regular cleaning, and the lines past it need jetting on a schedule that matches the volume of cooking. We help set up that rhythm so emergencies become scheduled visits.

Backflow devices on commercial supplies may need periodic testing. Rules and schedules are set by the water supplier and the state, so confirm with them what applies to your building. We can tell you what we have seen and point you to the right office.

Licensing and building permits.

Commercial plumbing in New Jersey must be performed by a licensed master plumber or their employees, as set out by the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. Larger projects may need permits and inspections from the City of Garfield building department at 111 Outwater Lane, and we handle the filing for work we perform.

If you are a tenant, check your lease for who is responsible for plumbing. A landlord's approval is often needed for repairs inside walls or on shared lines.
