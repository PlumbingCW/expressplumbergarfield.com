Diagnosis.

Sewer line repair starts with seeing the pipe.

Digging up a yard to find a break is the slow and costly way. A camera on a flexible cable shows the pipe from the inside: cracks, offset joints, roots, a belly that holds water and the exact distance to each problem.

Once we know what failed and where, we can match the fix to the damage. A short section may need only a spot repair. A long run of old pipe may justify replacement or lining.

Urgent signs.

When a damaged sewer line is an emergency.

  • Sewage is backing into the house and cleaning only helps for a few days.
  • A sinkhole, soft spot or wet strip shows up on the lawn along the pipe route.
  • A strong sewage smell in the yard or the cellar that does not fade.
  • Water meter shows steady use with no fixture running, which can point to water entering a damaged pipe.
  • The camera shows a collapsed section that blocks flow entirely.

Repair options compared.

The right choice depends on the pipe material, depth, what sits above it and how much damage the camera shows. We lay out the options with prices before anything starts.

What pipe lining is and when it fits.

Lining pushes a resin soaked tube into the old pipe and cures it into a smooth new pipe inside the old one. It seals cracks and joints without digging a trench. It needs a pipe that is still round and roughly in line, and it must be cleaned thoroughly first.

Lining does not fix a pipe that has collapsed, sagged badly or broken apart. In those cases, a section is dug out and replaced. The camera tells us which one applies, and we will show you the footage.

Garfield ground conditions.

Garfield sits beside the Passaic River and the Saddle River, and parts of the city are low. The National Weather Service records the Saddle River at Lodi cresting at 13.94 feet in September 1999 and 13.5 feet in August 2011, with flood stage at 6 feet. After floods like those, saturated ground can shift old sewer pipe and open joints.

That does not mean every Garfield sewer line is at risk. It means lines in low, wet areas deserve a camera look after any major flood, especially before a basement is finished.

Licensed work.

Sewer work in New Jersey must be done by a licensed master plumber or a contractor employed by one, according to the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. You can verify a plumber's license through the Division of Consumer Affairs before signing anything.
