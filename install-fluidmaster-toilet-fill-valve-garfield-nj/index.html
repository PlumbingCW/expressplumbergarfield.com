How to Install a Fluidmaster Toilet Fill Valve in Garfield, NJ.

To install a Fluidmaster toilet fill valve, you shut off the water at the stop valve, drain the tank, remove the old valve and set the new one in the same hole. Then you reconnect the supply line, clip the refill tube to the overflow tube and adjust the height. Most people finish in under an hour with a few basic tools.

This is one of the friendlier toilet repairs, and the steps below walk you through it. We also mark the points where it is smarter to stop and call. If water is spilling onto the floor, the 24/7 emergency line at (973) 488-5410 answers any time.

Diagnose first.

Is the fill valve really the problem?

The fill valve is the tall assembly on the left side of the tank that refills it after a flush. When it wears out, the toilet may run, hiss or fill slowly. A worn flapper at the bottom of the tank can cause a very similar running sound.

Before you buy parts, lift the tank lid and look. If water flows into the overflow tube, the fill valve is not shutting off. If water leaks from the tank into the bowl while the fill valve is quiet, suspect the flapper.

Before you begin.

What to gather before you start.

  • A new fill valve kit. Check that the box says it fits your tank height and supply line.
  • An adjustable wrench or pliers for the supply nut and the locknut under the tank.
  • A small bucket and a sponge or towels to catch water from the tank and the line.
  • A new braided supply line, if the old one is stiff, kinked or stained, since you will disconnect it anyway.
  • The manufacturer's instructions from the package. They cover fit details for your exact model.

Install the fill valve, step by step.

1.

Shut off and drain.

Turn the stop valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. Flush, hold the handle down and sponge out what remains in the tank.

2.

Disconnect the supply line.

Place the bucket under the connection. Unscrew the supply nut and the locknut under the tank, then lift the old valve out.

3.

Set the height.

Adjust the new valve so the top of the valve sits where the instructions say, relative to the overflow tube. Getting this right keeps the toilet from running.

4.

Seat the valve in the tank.

Drop the valve through the hole and tighten the locknut underneath. Keep it snug by hand and follow the box instructions, since too much force can crack the porcelain.

5.

Attach the refill tube.

Clip the small refill tube to the overflow tube so water flows into it and not across the tank. Reconnect the supply line to the bottom of the valve.

6.

Turn on slowly and test.

Open the stop valve a little, check for drips, then open it all the way. Adjust the float until the water stops a bit below the top of the overflow tube, and flush twice.

Dry the area with a towel and watch the connections for ten minutes. A bead of water at the nut means it needs another small turn.

Water on the floor and you cannot stop it?

Close the stop valve behind the toilet, or the main shutoff if it will not turn, and call the emergency line.

Know your limit.

When to stop and call a plumber.

  • The stop valve will not turn or leaks when you try. Forcing it can break the valve.
  • The tank is cracked or leaks from the base or the bolts. That is a bigger toilet repair, not a fill valve fix.
  • The locknut or supply nut is seized, corroded or starts to round off.
  • The toilet still runs after a correct install and a new flapper. Something else is wrong.
  • Water has reached the floor or ceiling below. Call about leak detection to check for hidden damage.
Local note.

Garfield tap water and your toilet's fill valve.

The City of Garfield's 2018 water quality report says the city blends its own wells with treated water bought from the Passaic Valley Water Commission. The north end of town gets mostly well water, the south end mostly Passaic Valley water and the middle a mix at the Belmont Hill Tank. The blend can differ from one street to the next, so test your tap and check what collects in the tank.

If you find grit or scale around the old valve, flush the supply line into the bucket for a few seconds before you connect the new one. Debris is a common reason a new valve hisses or fails to shut off. Ask your water utility if you want to know which supply serves your street.
