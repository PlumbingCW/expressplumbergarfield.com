Local reason.

Why Garfield basements need a plan for water.

Garfield sits between the Passaic River on one side and the Saddle River on another. The National Weather Service gauge on the Saddle River at Lodi recorded a crest of 13.94 feet in September 1999 during Hurricane Floyd and 13.5 feet in August 2011 during Hurricane Irene, against a flood stage of 6 feet.

Not every Garfield basement floods, but low ones near the rivers and the old flood paths do. If your cellar has a floor drain or a pit that has ever collected water, the pump is the thing that keeps the next storm from becoming a loss.

Urgent signs.

When a sump pump failure is an emergency.

  • The pit is full and the pump is silent, humming or running without moving water.
  • Water is on the floor and still rising.
  • The power is out during a storm, and the pump has no backup.
  • The pump runs constantly and cannot keep up.
  • You smell sewage or the pit is connected to a drain that is backing up.

Battery backups and why they matter.

The worst floods often come with power outages, which is exactly when an electric pump stops. A battery backup pump sits beside the main one and takes over when power fails or the main pump cannot keep up. A water powered backup works without electricity but needs municipal water pressure.

Backup systems need maintenance. Batteries lose capacity over a few years, and the system should be tested at least once each spring. We can show you how to test it in a few minutes.

Where the discharge water goes.

A pump moves water out. Where it goes matters. Discharge pipes should run away from the foundation and not toward a neighbor's property or into the sanitary sewer. Many municipalities prohibit connecting sump discharge to the sewer, and the rule can vary. Ask Garfield's building department what applies before we run the line.

A discharge that freezes in winter can back water into the pit. We choose routes and pipe slopes that drain fully when the pump stops.
