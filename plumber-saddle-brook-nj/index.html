Why Saddle Brook basements get wet.

Saddle Brook was named for the Saddle River, and the river is the reason many basements here need a plan. Hurricane Floyd hit in September 1999, and the National Weather Service gauge at Lodi, just downstream, recorded a crest of 13.94 feet, against a flood stage of 6 feet. Groundwater stays high for days afterward.

A battery backup on your sump pump is the single most useful upgrade for a basement near the river. See sump pump repair and installation for how we size and test them. According to the Saddle Brook Water Department's 2026 report, the township buys water from other systems including Veolia Hackensack and adds a corrosion inhibitor at the plant.

What counts as an emergency in Saddle Brook.

  • Water rising in a basement or around the foundation.
  • Sewage backing up through floor drains or fixtures.
  • A pipe leak that continues after the fixture valve is closed.
  • No heat or hot water in cold weather. See boiler repair.
  • A sump pump that has stopped or cannot keep up.

Test your pump before the next storm.

Pour a few buckets of water into the sump pit and watch the pump start. If it does not, call before the rain. A pump that is silent on a dry day will not save you on a wet one.

Reading the storm forecast like a plumber.

When the forecast calls for several inches of rain, treat it as a checklist day. Clear leaves from gutters, make sure downspouts discharge away from the foundation, lift stored boxes off the cellar floor and test the sump pump. Each step takes minutes and can save a basement.

If the pump fails the test, call before the storm arrives. Scheduling a replacement on a dry day is far easier than a night call while the pit is overflowing.
