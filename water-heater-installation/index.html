Planning first.

Water heater installation in Garfield, planned the right way.

A good installation begins before the heater arrives. Someone has to measure the space, confirm the fuel supply, check the flue or vent path and size the unit to the household. Skipping those steps is how a new heater ends up undersized, short on venting or too tall for the cellar ceiling.

Most Garfield installs happen in basements with low ceilings, older masonry chimneys and floor drains that have not been touched in decades. We plan around those realities and tell you early when something has to change. According to the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, water heater installation must be done by a licensed master plumber or someone working under one, so ask to see a license before you agree to a price.

Reasons people schedule a new installation.

Switching fuel.

Moving from electric to gas, or the reverse, changes the supply lines, the venting and sometimes the panel.

Moving the heater.

A renovated basement or a tenant unit may need the heater relocated, which means new water, vent and drain runs.

Right sizing.

A new bathroom, a larger family or a second kitchen can overwhelm a 30 gallon tank.

Going tankless.

On demand heaters need the right gas line, venting and condensate drain. See tankless water heater service.

New construction or additions.

A gut renovation of a two family house often adds a second water heater.

Code and safety upgrades.

Expansion tanks, discharge piping and seismic or flood protections matter as much as the unit itself.

Tank or tankless for planned work.

Tank water heater.

  • Lower upfront price and simple installation.
  • Works with most existing gas and electric supplies.
  • Holds a limited supply that can run out.
  • Needs floor space and a safe discharge path.

Tankless water heater.

  • Heats on demand and can free floor space.
  • Needs a gas line and venting sized for the load.
  • Benefits from regular flushing in hard water.
  • Higher upfront cost and more installation detail.
How it goes.

From planning call to final test.

1.

Site visit or detailed phone review.

We confirm the location, fuel, venting path, ceiling height and what the household really uses.

2.

Written scope and price.

You get a single price that lists the unit, the labor, the venting and any code items.

3.

Installation day.

Old connections are removed or capped, the new heater is set and the water, fuel and vent lines are made up.

4.

Testing and handoff.

We test for leaks, combustion on gas units, temperature setting and discharge, then show you the shutoff valves.

What Garfield basements teach us about venting.

Many older Garfield houses were built when heaters vented into masonry chimneys. A newer high efficiency heater may not use that chimney at all, and an oversized old flue can cause poor draft. Checking the vent path early prevents carbon monoxide risks and failed inspections.

If your cellar floods in heavy rain, we raise the heater on a stand where possible. The National Weather Service's records for the Saddle River gauge at Lodi list crests of 13.94 feet during Hurricane Floyd in September 1999 and 13.5 feet during Hurricane Irene in August 2011, against a flood stage of 6 feet. Garfield shares that river with Wallington, so low basements deserve a plan.
