Sink Garbage Disposal Repair in Garfield, NJ: Jams, Resets, Leaks and Replacement.

Most garbage disposal repair starts with three checks: turn the power off, free the jam from underneath with a hex wrench, and press the red reset button. A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed, and one that is silent has usually tripped its reset. A leak is different and often means the unit or a seal has failed.

Never put your hand in a disposal, even when it is off. In Garfield, NJ, our emergency line is open 24/7 if a leak is flooding the kitchen. For anything else, here is what to try and when to stop.

Start here.

Clear a jam and reset the unit.

1.

Cut the power.

Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit, or turn off the breaker if it is hard wired.

2.

Look from above.

Use a flashlight to check for a spoon, bone or fruit pit. Remove anything with tongs, never your hand.

3.

Turn the hex wrench.

Insert the wrench into the socket in the center bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until it turns freely.

4.

Press reset.

Push the red button on the bottom of the unit once the motor has cooled.

5.

Test with water.

Run cold water, restore power and switch the disposal on briefly.

If the unit trips again right away, do not keep pressing reset. The motor may be failing.

Finding where the leak comes from.

Dry the cabinet, wipe the unit and tape a sheet of paper towel under it. Run water and watch where the first drop appears. A leak from the top flange points to the sink mounting. A drip at the dishwasher hose or drain pipe points to a loose connection.

A leak from the body of the disposal itself means a cracked housing or failed internal seal. That cannot be fixed with a wrench. If you are not sure, our team can sort out the source, and our garbage disposal page explains the service. The City of Garfield 2018 water quality report marks hardness as above the recommended range and says hardness scales pipes. Scale can narrow the drain line past the disposal, so slow draining may not be the disposal at all. Run cold water, and if the sink still backs up, ask us about the line.

Many older houses in Garfield have drain lines that have seen decades of use. A disposal can push grease and scraps into those lines, so keep fats and starchy scraps out. If a clog follows, see our drain cleaning service.

Habits that prevent jams.

Do not put these down a disposal.

  • Grease and oil, which cool and coat the pipes.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts, which pack into sludge.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and clog.
  • Bones and fruit pits, which jam the flywheel.
  • Fibrous scraps such as corn husks and celery strings.

Run cold water for several seconds before and after each use, so scraps move all the way past the trap. A few ice cubes ground up once in a while knock off residue on the grinding parts, and citrus peels freshen the chamber.

Repair or replace the disposal.

Ask for a written price on both options before work starts, so you can compare them side by side.

Repair usually makes sense when.

  • The unit jammed once and now runs fine.
  • The leak is at a hose, flange or drain connection.
  • The motor is quiet and the reset holds.

Replace usually makes sense when.

  • The housing leaks from the body.
  • The reset trips again and again.
  • The grinding parts are worn and noisy.

When the problem is not the disposal.

A disposal that runs fine but leaves the sink full is often a clog downstream. Check the other side of a double sink and the trap under the cabinet. If both sides back up together, the blockage sits farther along the line.

Cold water matters here, because it keeps grease solid so the blades can chop it and the flow can carry it away. Warm water melts fats that then coat the pipe farther down.

A disposal that smells even after cleaning may have residue under the splash guard. Lift the rubber flange and scrub the underside with a brush and dish soap. If the sink also gurgles, the vent or drain line may need attention, and a plumber can scope it.

Wiring, permits and who should do the work.

A disposal runs on household power, so replacing one involves electrical connections as well as plumbing. In New Jersey, plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber or people working under one. You can look up a plumber through the Division of Consumer Affairs.
