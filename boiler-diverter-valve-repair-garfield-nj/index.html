Boiler Diverter Valve Repair for Garfield, NJ Homes: Which Diverter Do You Have.

The words diverter valve mean two different things in a house. One is the tub spout or shower diverter that sends water to the showerhead. The other is a valve inside or beside a boiler that sends hot water either to the heating loop or to your taps. Boiler diverter valve repair in Garfield, NJ starts with working out which one you have.

If your problem is heat, hot water at the tap or a boiler error code, the part is likely in the boiler. If water keeps pouring from the tub spout while the shower is on, it is the bath diverter. Our emergency line runs 24/7 if a boiler fails in cold weather.

Two parts, one name.

Many combination boilers, often called combi boilers, use a three way diverter valve to switch between heat and hot water. Other systems use zone valves or an indirect tank, which work differently.

Tub and shower diverter.

  • Sits in the tub spout or as a handle on the wall.
  • Sends water up to the showerhead when lifted or turned.
  • Failure shows as water dripping from the spout during a shower.
  • Usually a fixture repair or a spout replacement.

Boiler diverter valve.

  • Sits in the boiler or in the piping near it.
  • Directs hot water to heating or to domestic hot water.
  • Failure shows as no heat, no hot water or odd temperatures.
  • A heating repair that needs a plumber or boiler technician.
Boiler side.

Signs a boiler diverter valve is failing.

Not every one of these points to the diverter. Air, low pressure or a failed pump can look the same.

Heat but no hot tap water.

The valve may be stuck on the heating side.

Hot tap water but cold radiators.

The valve may be stuck toward the domestic side.

Both lukewarm.

A worn valve can leak between the two paths.

Clicking or humming at the valve.

The motor or actuator may be struggling.

Error codes on the boiler display.

Check your manual, since each maker uses its own codes.

Before you call.

What a homeowner can safely check.

  • Look at the boiler pressure gauge and compare it with the range in the manual.
  • Check that the thermostat is calling for heat and that its batteries are fresh.
  • Make sure the boiler has power and the switch is on.
  • Look for water under or around the unit.
  • Write down any error code and the time it appeared.

Do not open the boiler cabinet or drain the system. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

Fixing a tub spout diverter.

If the problem is at the bath, check the pull-up knob on the spout. A worn gasket inside lets water slip past it. Many spouts unscrew by hand or with a set screw at the bottom, and a new spout is a simple swap.

When the shower has its own valve with a diverter cartridge behind the handle, replacing the cartridge is the fix. Shut off the water first and take a photo before you start. For deeper leaks inside the wall, our faucet repair page explains when to call. According to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs, you can look up a plumber's license online before you hire, and we suggest you do that for any contractor who opens a boiler.

Older Garfield houses and boiler heat.

Many older houses in Garfield heat with a boiler in the cellar, and many are one, two or three family buildings. In a multi-unit house, ask which tenant or owner controls the boiler before you call, so the visit goes quickly.

The process.

What a boiler diverter repair visit involves.

1.

Confirm the fault.

The technician tests the thermostat call, pump and valve motor to rule out other causes.

2.

Isolate the system.

Power and water to the section are shut off so the valve can be handled safely.

3.

Replace the part.

The valve body or its actuator is replaced, depending on which one failed.

4.

Bleed and test.

Air is purged, pressure is checked and both heating and hot water are tested.

What to tell the technician on the phone.

Say whether you have a combi boiler, a boiler with a separate hot water tank or a steam system. Tell us the make if you know it, and describe exactly what is hot and what is cold. A clear description lets us bring the right valve or actuator on the first visit.
