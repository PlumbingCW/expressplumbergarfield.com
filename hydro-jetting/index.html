Emergency Hydro Jetting in Garfield, NJ.

Hydro jetting clears a drain line with high pressure water instead of a cable, and it is the next step when a backup keeps coming back. We take emergency jetting calls in Garfield 24/7 for sewer backups that a cable could not fix.

Until we arrive, stop using water in the house, keep drains closed and move belongings away from basement floor drains. Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher.

Hydro jetting: what the machine does.

A jetting hose carries water at high pressure to a nozzle with forward and rear facing jets. The rear jets pull the hose through the pipe, and the forward jet breaks up the blockage. The spray also scrubs the pipe wall, which a cable cannot do.

That scrubbing is the point. A cable punches a hole through a clog and leaves the grease and scale on the walls. The hole closes again over weeks. Jetting takes the buildup off the walls, so the pipe is close to its full diameter again.

Urgent steps.

Sewer backup: what to do first.

  • Stop the water. No sinks, showers, washing machines or flushing until the line is open.
  • Close the lids and plugs on floor drains and tubs that may back up.
  • Keep people and pets out of any area where sewage has come up.
  • Do not pour bleach or cleaners into the backed up fixture.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer before cleanup starts.

Once the water is down, we jet the line and check the result with a camera if you want proof the pipe is clear.

When jetting is the right tool.

Grease buildup.

Restaurant lines and older kitchen drains collect grease that cables cannot remove.

Tree roots.

Fine root hairs growing through a joint can be cut and flushed out, though a damaged joint still needs repair.

Scale and sludge.

Years of hard water minerals and soap residue narrow the pipe.

Repeat backups.

If a cable fixed it only for a few weeks, the walls are the problem.

Before relining.

A pipe must be cleaned thoroughly before a liner goes in. See sewer line repair.

Cable or jetting.

Neither tool fixes a broken pipe. If the line is damaged, we say so and show it on camera.

Cable machine.

  • Good for a single soft clog.
  • Quick and lower cost.
  • Leaves buildup on pipe walls.
  • Can snag in a broken or offset pipe.

Hydro jetting.

  • Cleans the full circumference of the pipe.
  • Best for grease, scale and root hairs.
  • Needs a clear cleanout to feed the hose.
  • Not suited to a pipe that is already cracked or collapsing.

When we do not jet.

Jetting pushes high pressure water into a pipe, so the pipe has to be sound. Brittle cast iron, a cracked clay line or a joint that has dropped can be damaged by the spray. That is why we often run a camera first on older Garfield lines and decide on the evidence.

If the camera shows a break, a belly that holds water or a collapsed section, the job moves to sewer line repair. Cleaning a broken pipe only delays the failure.

What a jetting visit includes.

1.

Find the access point.

A cleanout in the cellar or yard is best. If none exists, we explain the options for adding one.

2.

Pre check.

A cable or camera identifies the obstruction and the condition of the pipe.

3.

Jet the line.

We feed the hose and nozzle in, working back and forth until water runs clear.

4.

Confirm.

We flush the line and, if you want, record a camera pass after cleaning.
