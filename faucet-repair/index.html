Faucet repair in Garfield, from drip to flood.

A slow drip wastes water and wears out a fixture. A faucet that will not shut off at all, or a supply line that has split under the sink, is different. It needs to be stopped now, because water under a cabinet soaks the floor and the wall behind it.

We start by stopping the flow. After that we work out whether the faucet needs a cartridge, a seat and spring set, an aerator, new supply lines or a full replacement. The answer depends on the brand, the age and how much scale is inside. According to the City of Garfield's 2018 water quality report, hardness is above the recommended range, so scale on cartridges and aerators is the first thing we look for in a stubborn faucet.

Urgent signs.

When a faucet problem is an emergency.

  • The faucet will not turn off and the shutoff valves under the sink will not close.
  • A supply line is spraying or the braided hose has burst.
  • Water drips from the cabinet and the floor is already wet.
  • A handle or body has come loose and water leaks at the base.
  • The sink has no hot water and the household needs it, such as with a baby at home.

Scale and Garfield faucets.

The City of Garfield's 2018 water quality report flags hardness above the recommended range. In a kitchen that shows up as white crust on the spout, a weak stream and cartridges that stick. Soaking an aerator in vinegar clears light buildup.

A faucet that has been scaled for years may be cheaper to replace than to repair, and old shutoff valves under the sink often fail when turned. We replace them at the same visit, because a working shutoff is what turns the next leak into a ten second fix.

Repair or replace the faucet.

Repair makes sense when the faucet is sound and parts are available. Replacement makes sense when the body is corroded, parts are discontinued or the finish is worn through. A new faucet also brings a modern flow rate, which cuts water use.

If you are remodeling a kitchen or bath, see plumbing installation for fixtures, valves and new supply runs. For disposal trouble under the sink, see garbage disposal repair.
